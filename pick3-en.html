How Florida Pick 3 Works

Pick 3 is one of the most popular games of the Florida Lottery. It is simple: you choose three digits from 0 to 9 and hope they match the three digits drawn. This guide explains how it works, what play types exist and, above all, what the real odds are.

When the draws take place

Florida holds two Pick 3 draws every day: one at midday (around 1:30 PM Eastern Time) and one in the evening (around 9:45 PM). Each draw is independent: what came out at midday has no effect on the evening draw.

How to play

The player picks three digits, for example 4-1-7, then chooses the amount to wager and the type of play. Three balls are then drawn, one for each position, from separate machines holding the digits 0 to 9. Because digits can repeat, there are exactly 1,000 possible combinations, from 000 to 999.

Most common play types

  • Straight (exact): all three digits must come out in the same order you chose. It is the hardest play and the one that pays the most.
  • Box: you win if the three digits come out in any order. It pays less than straight, but it is easier to hit.
  • Straight/Box: combines both. If it comes out in exact order you collect more; in any other order you collect the box prize.
  • Pair plays: in some options you can bet only on the first two or the last two digits.

Prizes change with the amount wagered and the play type. Always check the official prize table at www.flalottery.com, the only reliable source.

The real odds

This is where many people get confused. The odds are mathematical; they do not depend on luck of the day or on any "system".

PlayApproximate odds
Straight (exact order)1 in 1,000
Box with 3 different digits (6 orders)1 in 167
Box with one repeated digit (3 orders)1 in 333

This means that, on average, a given straight number comes out once every 1,000 draws. If a number has not come out in 200 draws, that does not make it "more likely": every draw starts from zero.

Myths to avoid

  • "A number that is overdue must come out soon": false, the balls have no memory.
  • "There is a system to predict the result": no tool, including number generators, can predict a draw.
  • "Playing more greatly increases my chances": each extra play buys one more chance, but the total cost also rises.

Play responsibly

The lottery should be seen as entertainment, not a source of income. Set a spending limit before you play, do not use money meant for basic expenses and do not try to win back losses by betting more. Only people 18 or older may play. If you feel gambling is getting out of hand, seek professional help.
