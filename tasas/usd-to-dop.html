The dollar is the currency most closely watched in the Dominican Republic: it affects the price of fuel, appliances, travel and a large share of imports. Dominicans living in the United States also send money home every month, so the USD/DOP rate matters to both the sender and the receiver.

Exchange offices and banks publish their own buy and sell rates, which are almost always different from the international reference rate. Use this page to see roughly what your exchange should be worth, then compare it with what you are offered before you decide.

How to calculate how many Dominican pesos your dollars are worth

Multiply the amount in dollars by the USD/DOP rate and you have the pesos. The calculator does it instantly, and the swap button shows how many dollars you would need to reach a given amount in pesos.

A small gap in the rate becomes a big gap on a large amount: the same difference barely shows on 50 dollars, but it is clearly visible on 5,000. Before you exchange a large sum, ask for the exact rate and do the maths.

Also remember that buyers and sellers of dollars do not use the same price. When you sell dollars you are paid the buy rate; when you buy them you pay the sell rate, which is usually higher. The rate on this page sits roughly in the middle.

Tips before you exchange dollars for pesos

  • Exchange at banks or licensed exchange offices that show the day's rate clearly.
  • Airport and tourist-area exchange counters often have wider margins, so compare first when you can.
  • Bring bills in good condition: many places turn down torn, stained or very worn dollars.
  • If you are receiving a remittance, ask whether it will be paid in pesos or dollars and at what rate, then compare the final amount with what the sender was told.
  • Count your money and check the receipt before you walk away.

USD to DOP: frequently asked questions

Why does the dollar move against the peso?
It depends on supply and demand for foreign currency, inflation, interest rates and the economic situation in both countries, so it can change from one day to the next.

Which rate should I use to estimate a remittance?
The one the service you are using applies. This page is only a reference to judge whether an offer looks reasonable.

Is a bank or an exchange office better?
There is no fixed rule. It depends on the day, the amount and the fee, so compare at least two options.

How much is 100 dollars in pesos?
Check the quick table on this page or type 100 into the calculator; it uses the day's reference rate.
