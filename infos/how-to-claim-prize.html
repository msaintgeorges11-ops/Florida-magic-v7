How to Claim a Florida Lottery Prize: Locations and Deadlines

Think you won the Florida Lottery? Before you celebrate, you need to know where to go, which documents to bring and how long you have to act. A winning ticket that is not claimed in time is lost, with no exceptions. This guide summarizes the main steps for claiming your prize safely.

The Deadlines

For draw games such as Pick 3, Pick 4, Fantasy 5, Powerball or Mega Millions, the prize must be claimed within 180 days of the drawing date. For scratch-off games, the deadline is 60 days after the official end of the game. Do not wait until the last minute: the longer you wait, the greater the risk of losing or damaging your ticket.

Where to Claim by Prize Amount

Prize amountWhere to claim
Up to $599Any authorized retailer
$600 or moreA Florida Lottery district office, or by mail
Top prizes (over $1 million, or annuity payments)Lottery headquarters in Tallahassee

These rules can change, and some offices ask you to book an appointment. Always check the official Florida Lottery page before you travel.

Required Documents

  • Your winning ticket, signed on the back.
  • The Winner Claim Form for prizes of $600 or more.
  • A valid government-issued photo ID.
  • Your Social Security number or taxpayer identification number.

Prizes of $600 or more are reported to the IRS. Authorities may also check whether you owe debts to the state, which can be deducted from your prize.

Steps to Follow

  • Check your ticket carefully, online, with the official app or at a retailer.
  • Sign the back of the ticket right away and take a photo of it.
  • Fill out the claim form if the prize reaches $600.
  • Go to the right place with your documents, or send them by tracked mail.

Mistakes to Avoid

The first mistake is not signing the ticket: an unsigned ticket can be claimed by anyone. The second is folding, wetting or damaging the barcode, which complicates verification. Finally, never hand your ticket to a stranger and never send money to "release" a prize: that is a classic scam.

If You Lose Your Ticket

A lost or stolen ticket is very hard to recover, because it belongs to whoever holds it. That is why the photo taken right after purchase and the signature on the back matter so much.

Claiming by Mail

If you cannot travel, you can send your signed ticket, the form and a copy of your ID by mail. Always use tracked delivery with a return receipt, and keep a photo of the front and back of the ticket before sending it. Check the exact address on the official website.

Prepare in Advance

Before buying a ticket, note the date of each drawing and set a reminder to check your results. Keep your tickets in an envelope or sleeve. If you win a large amount, take the time to consult a financial advisor before deciding what to do next. There is no rush to spend, but there is a deadline to claim.

An Important Reminder

Gambling should remain a pastime. Spend only what you can afford to lose, avoid playing when emotional or tired, and review your spending regularly. If playing starts to cause stress, conflict or financial difficulty, talk to someone you trust or to a specialized support service. It is reserved for adults.

Summary

You have 180 days after a drawing to claim your prize. Up to $599, a retailer is enough; from $600, you need a Lottery office or mail. Sign your ticket, gather your documents and always check the official rules. Play in moderation.
